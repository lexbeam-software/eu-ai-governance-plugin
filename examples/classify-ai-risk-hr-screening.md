# Example: HR resume-screening classification

> Fictional demonstration. Verify the intended purpose, system version, contracts, national law, and current official text before reliance.

## Executive classification

**High-risk under Article 6(2) and Annex III point 4(a).** The fictional system analyses and filters job applications and automatically rejects applicants below a threshold. No Article 5 prohibition is established on the supplied facts. Article 50 and GDPR duties require separate analysis.

## Facts and assumptions

- The system scores applications and rejects the bottom 40% before recruiter review.
- It is used by German and Austrian employers.
- A third-party vendor markets the system under its own name; the employers use it under their authority.
- No biometric, emotion-recognition, or synthetic-content function is established.
- The output materially influences recruitment decisions and profiles applicants.

Missing evidence includes model and data documentation, bias testing, instructions, logs, provider role, training-data use, applicant notices, national worker-representation process, and human-review design.

## Actor-specific obligations

Provider evidence should cover Articles 9 to 17, documentation keeping under Article 18, provider logs under Article 19, corrective action, conformity assessment under Article 43, declaration under Article 47, CE marking under Article 48, registration under Article 49, post-market monitoring under Article 72, and serious incidents under Article 73, subject to applicability and the operative date.

Deployer controls should cover Article 26 instructions, competent human oversight, relevant and sufficiently representative input data where controlled, monitoring and suspension, at least six months of logs under its control unless other law provides otherwise, workplace notice under Article 26(7), DPIA linkage under Article 26(9), and affected-person notice under Article 26(11).

An Article 27 FRIA is **not automatically mandatory for every private employer**. It applies only to the actors and systems listed in Article 27(1). The employers in this fictional example are not described as public-law bodies or private entities providing public services, and Annex III 4(a) is not one of the specific financial use cases in Article 27(1). A voluntary fundamental-rights assessment may still be valuable.

Run a GDPR Article 35 trigger analysis separately. Automated applicant rejection makes a DPIA highly likely on these facts, but the controller must document the legal test and applicable supervisory guidance.

## DACH escalation

For Germany, analyse Article 26(7) and the applicable BetrVG information and co-determination provisions against the system’s monitoring and selection functions. For Austria, conduct the separate national employee-representation and data-protection analysis. Do not label one result “mandatory in DACH.”
